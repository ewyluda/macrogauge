## P2 findings

### F1 — Distinct events collapse into the same story

Location: `site/src/lib/newsTape.ts:182–188`, particularly the unconditional figure match at line 187.

Once two posts share any ticker and occur within 96 hours, an equal extracted number is sufficient to declare them the same story. A synthetic Microsoft $5B nuclear-electricity contract and an unrelated Microsoft $5B cybersecurity acquisition return `sameStory = true`. The fallback also merges “Microsoft opens 200 MW data center in Texas” with “Microsoft opens 900 MW data center in Finland”: the tokenizer drops short numeric tokens and the remaining shared boilerplate clears the 0.3 threshold.

This removes distinct events from the headline list, understates story counts, combines ticker/layer classifications, and gives only one event the lead and headline-number slot. The Everything mode applies the same clustering, so it cannot restore independent rows. These are controlled reproduction examples, not claims about actual Microsoft announcements.

Require corroborating event context (project/site, counterparties, action) in addition to matching amounts. Treat conflicting locations and capacities as reasons to keep events separate. Add negative clustering fixtures; the current positive examples and count-preservation test do not establish event identity.

### F2 — A newer cancellation can remain hidden behind an older announcement

Location: `site/src/lib/newsTape.ts:199–204`.

`leadRank` gives casing and the presence of supporting points absolute precedence over time. In a controlled pair, an October 6 prose announcement that Microsoft secured a 500 MW nuclear-power agreement stays the lead after an October 7 all-capitals flash says the same agreement was cancelled. The newer flash is folded into `also`. `/datacenter` provides no way to expand it, and the biggest-numbers strip can continue to feature the older agreement.

The follow-up commit correctly distinguishes the lead's timestamp from the update time, but it does not correct which development is presented. Prefer the latest substantive development; use formatting/detail quality as a tie-breaker for equivalent reports, or expose the newer update prominently. Cover cancellation, denial, and revised terms in tests.

### F3 — Folded posts lose their supporting details in both modes

Location: `site/src/components/NewsFeed.tsx:143–146`.

The repeat renderer outputs only timestamp, headline, and source link. Its `points`, ticker tags, and `impacted` fields have no rendering path. This is observable in the committed tape: Micron post `3AE4E2920EF79F8855F5` folds behind `3A7F621BC65A120A9A5C`. The repeat contains fiscal Q1 revenue guidance of $61.5B, EPS guidance of $38.15, tax assumptions, and DRAM/NAND outlook details that are absent from the lead's points. These values are cited as fixture contents, not independently verified financial facts.

Opening both Details and “1 more post” in the browser exposes only the lead's points and repeat headline. Rendering the same pair from the updated commit confirms the missing guidance is absent from markup, not merely collapsed. Switching to Everything does not recover it.

Render each repeat's original details under its own disclosure, or provide a raw-post view. Test preservation of unique supporting content, not just the number of repeat `<li>` elements.

### F4 — Grouped decimal capacity is silently truncated

Location: `site/src/lib/newsTape.ts:113` and `131–134`.

The capacity regex permits only one comma or decimal segment. For the valid headline quantity `1,250.5 MW`, matching restarts after the comma and captures `250.5 MW`. The function returns 250.5 and the UI labels it `251 MW`, instead of representing the original 1,250.5 MW. This also corrupts figure-based story matching because clustering uses the extracted value.

Parse the complete numeric token, supporting grouped thousands plus a decimal part, and prevent partial matching inside malformed or unsupported numbers. Add assertions on numeric values as well as display labels.

### F5 — Revenue and valuation amounts are accepted as deal dollars

Location: `site/src/lib/newsTape.ts:136–140`; selection at `227–231`.

`DEAL_WORDS` qualifies an entire sentence, after which every dollar amount in that sentence is classified as a deal. The synthetic headline “Microsoft reported $100 billion of revenue and signed a $5 billion data center financing deal” yields both amounts; `topFigures` then presents $100B as Deal dollars because it chooses the first amount. The checked-in Lambda headline also produces both its $4B funding amount and $14.5B pre-money valuation as dollar figures, although its current ordering keeps the strip's selected value correct.

This defeats the stated revenue exclusion and can inflate both the displayed deal size and number-based clustering matches. Associate each amount with its own clause/role and exclude revenue, valuation, price targets, and unrelated historical amounts. A sentence-level deal-word check is insufficient.

### F6 — The forecast guard drops signed contracts and completed projects

Location: `site/src/lib/newsTape.ts:117` and `130`.

`FORECAST` treats the noun “project” and any “through 20xx” phrase as forecasting, then discards every figure in that sentence. The real baked Black Hills/Google post `3A00216F4A1F698DA7A8` states that definitive agreements were signed **through 2048**, with **590 MW** of grid service and **2.1 GW** of third-party resources. `figures` returns `[]`, so the 590 MW first figure is omitted from the top-four capacity strip while the smaller 240 MW story appears. The date describes contract duration; it does not turn the signed agreement into a forecast.

A controlled completed-project headline also loses its 500 MW figure. Distinguish forecasting verbs from project nouns and contract dates, and apply exclusions to the relevant claim rather than every number in a sentence. The follow-up's month-May correction does not address these cases.

### F7 — Substantive short wire flashes are still treated as stubs

Location: `site/src/lib/newsTape.ts:57–59`.

The follow-up recognizes named subjects for short headlines with supporting points, but its next condition unconditionally rejects every headline-only post with fewer than eight words and no digits. `NVIDIA HALTS GPU SHIPMENTS` with `points: []` is therefore a stub despite naming a company, action, and infrastructure product. Because `noiseReason` is also a clustering veto, the flash cannot even join a fuller report of the same event.

This suppresses actionable wire updates from the default view and datacenter strip. Apply the substantive-subject/action check consistently to the headline-only path, and test short flashes with `points: []`; the new “Micron raises HBM guidance” test inherits nonempty points from its helper and misses this branch.

### F8 — The smoke test assumes the daily tape has a first-page repeat and a nonempty infra pool

Location: `site/e2e/smoke.spec.ts:623–629`; also `619` and `646`.

The test searches the whole infra pool for the first folded story, then tries to interact with that row before loading beyond the first 40 stories. A valid generated fixture with 41 newer independent stories and one older repeated story puts the first repeat at index 41. The test's target is absent from the initial story rows, even though the page is behaving correctly.

The test also unconditionally expects a mode note on an empty tape, although the component deliberately omits it, and dereferences `infra[0].lead` when no infra stories exist. These cases can falsely fail CI after a routine data publish; the repository runs site CI after daily workflow completion.

Use deterministic fixtures for semantic assertions. For a changing-tape smoke test, select a visible repeat or page until it is present, and explicitly handle zero-post/zero-infra states. The independent script proves the page/fixture mismatch through server rendering; it does not claim to have run Playwright against the synthetic fixture.

### F10 — All-capitals MAY forecasts now enter the numbers strip

Location: `site/src/lib/newsTape.ts:117–119` and `130`.

The follow-up removes case-insensitive `may` from the forecast expression and replaces it with a case-sensitive lowercase-only regex to preserve the month May. Consequently, `MICROSOFT MAY ADD 500 MW OF DATA CENTER CAPACITY IN TEXAS` yields a 500 MW figure, whereas the equivalent lowercase forecast is excluded. Uppercase wire headlines are explicitly supported elsewhere in this module and present in the tape.

This introduces speculative capacity/deal amounts into a strip claiming to exclude forecasts. Disambiguate the month using context, while retaining case-insensitive recognition of the modal verb. Test uppercase, lowercase, and title-case forecasts alongside month references.

## P3 finding

### F9 — Transitive matches exceed the documented four-day story window

Location: `site/src/lib/newsTape.ts:211–215`, using the pairwise check at `183`.

A post can join a group if it matches any member. Reports on October 7, October 4, and October 1 therefore form one cluster: both adjacent gaps are three days, but the resulting story spans six days. The page describes a four-day window, and the tests verify only two-post separation. This permits stale events to remain folded into a newer story through an intermediate report.

Enforce a cluster-level maximum span or anchor comparison to the newest post. If rolling chains are intentional, describe that behavior and test it explicitly rather than presenting four days as the cluster limit.
