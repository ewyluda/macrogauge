## P2 — Separate gas-generation MW from mixed-resource service MW before calculating burn

Location: `pipeline/publish/gas.py:261-265` and 276-279.

`ai_gas_deals` treats every `mw_total` on a broadly tagged `technology == "gas"` deal as gas-fired output. That field can instead be contracted electric service or a resource bundle. The publisher drops `mw_basis` and the source notes that explain this distinction, then reports the entire amount as gas-fired MW and Bcf/day.

This is present in the committed artifact, not just a hypothetical config. Google/Black Hills contributes **590 MW / 0.103 Bcf/d**, although [Black Hills' release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/06/3376007/10381/en/black-hills-corp-executes-definitive-agreements-to-serve-google-s-planned-data-center-in-cheyenne-wyoming.html) identifies **564 MW** of gas generation plus **26 MW** of market purchases/tariff service. At this PR's heat rate, the stated gas nameplate corresponds to **0.099 Bcf/d**. TeraWulf contributes **1,000 MW / 0.175 Bcf/d** of gas output, but [Kentucky Power's release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/kentucky-power-terawulf-finalize-expanded-agreement-providing-for-100-million-in-customer-benefits-302898378.html) distinguishes the 1,000 MW service contract from the **760 MW** planned combined-cycle plant, corresponding to **0.133 Bcf/d**. These source distinctions were also already recorded in the repository's config notes. Amazon's 3,000 MW bundle likewise carries a note that it includes more than the CCGT; do not assume the unverified component split is established.

Add source-supported gas-generation MW or explicit component allocations, and use those for the burn calculation and aggregation. Unknown gas allocations should remain unknown. Alternatively, label a separate all-service-at-gas counterfactual as such; it is not the gas output of the announced plants. Full-output and heat-rate caveats do not resolve a capacity-basis mismatch. Test mixed-resource, contracted-load and pure-gas-nameplate examples.

## P2 — Limit the headline to the Henry Hub comparison it actually measures

Location: `site/src/lib/gasHeadline.ts:35-40`; related explanation in `site/src/app/gas/page.tsx:81-87`.

When power rises by at least 10% and Henry Hub rises less than half as much, the helper concludes: “The fuel did not move the price; the grid did.” The writer has no local delivered-gas prices or attribution model, so the ratio does not establish this causal conclusion. Comparing the same hub with its prior-year ratio does not hold local gas basis constant. The lower-page local-basis caveat contradicts, rather than validates, the definitive takeaway. The committed page currently renders this claim for PJM.

The probe executes the actual TypeScript helper with power increasing from $60 to $90/MWh and Henry Hub flat at $3/MMBtu. Local gas increasing from $8 to $12/MMBtu at an unchanged 7.5 MMBtu/MWh heat rate explains the entire power increase with zero grid markup in either period. The helper nevertheless blames the grid. Describe power outpacing Henry Hub and leave local gas, generation mix and grid factors unresolved unless separately measured. Test this local-basis counterexample and a case where Henry Hub rises materially but less than half as much as power.

## P3 — Require twelve consecutive forecast months before publishing the next-year average

Location: `pipeline/publish/gas.py:103-108`.

`rows[:12]` checks the count of available observations, not whether they represent the next twelve calendar months. The EIA connector skips null values, and the store/schema permit gaps. With November 2026 absent, twelve available observations run from October 2026 through October 2027. The writer still publishes `next12_avg`, and `fuel_cost` uses it for the next-12-month fuel table. In the controlled fixture, eleven prices of $3 plus an October 2027 price of $30 produce **$5.25**, where the intended October 2026–September 2027 horizon is incomplete. Filling November restores **$3.00** and the correct September endpoint.

Construct the intended calendar window, require every month, and return null for its average if incomplete. The current committed forecast is complete; this is a confirmed missing-data defect, not an assertion that its current $3.11 average is wrong.

## P3 — Do not label a multiweek storage difference as a weekly change

Location: `pipeline/publish/gas.py:139-142`.

The previous available row is used without checking its date. If October 2 is absent, September 25's 3,400 Bcf and October 9's 3,567 Bcf publish `chg_week_bcf: 167`, which the page labels as the change “on the week.” That is a two-week movement. Adding the missing 3,500 Bcf October 2 observation changes the true one-week result to **67 Bcf**. Resolve the observation exactly seven days earlier or publish null, and retain a normal adjacent-week control. The committed artifact currently has consecutive observations and is unaffected.
