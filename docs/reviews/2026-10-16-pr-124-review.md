## Finding

### 1. P2 — #124 still treats faster hiring as proof of subcontractor scarcity

**Location:** `site/src/app/markets/page.tsx:90–93`.

The revised paragraph still concludes that contractors adding workers twice as fast as the country means subcontractor coverage is thinning. Employment growth alone cannot identify excess demand over available labor. It can also reflect successful recruitment, labor inflows, recovery from a small base, or projects in other construction sectors. The PR's improved contractor-location caveat makes direct attribution to the selected data-center sites less defensible, not more.

This is a **retained methodological assumption in rewritten copy**, not a newly introduced calculation regression. For example, a market with 2% job growth versus 1% nationally can have either falling or rising utilization; the page has no utilization, vacancy, unfilled-bid, or labor-hours-demand input to distinguish them.

**Correction:** describe faster hiring as a signal of construction activity or possible competition for labor. Reserve an assertion of tightness for corroborating evidence such as wage pressure, vacancy duration, overtime, subcontractor bids, or project labor demand. The [BLS worksite reporting guidance](https://www.bls.gov/respondents/mwr/questions-and-answers.htm) supports the new location caveat; it does not support the scarcity inference.

## Direction and passing controls
