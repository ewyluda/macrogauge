## Findings, in severity order

### 1. P2 — #125 permanently excludes three counties instead of allowing disclosure recovery

**Location:** `tests/test_dc_markets_config.py:63–73`, `tests/test_registry.py:193–203`, and the new `QCEW_238222` registrations in `config/series.json:860–942`. The new tests explicitly lock in the omissions. The collector's existing requested-ID filter makes this a collection exclusion, not just a display caveat.

Richland Parish LA (`22083`), Storey County NV (`32029`), and Grant County WA (`53025`) are absent from the registry because no historical mechanical-trade quarter was disclosed in the audited window. If BLS later discloses any of those cells, the pipeline still discards them before storing anything. Richland and Quincy remain unavailable; Reno remains partial with Storey listed as suppressed. No freshness policy or review deadline applies to an unregistered series.

**Controlled reproduction:** replace the three suppressed/missing cells in the committed 238222 fixture with valid, disclosed copies of the Loudoun county row. Run the actual registry-driven collector. All three counties produce **zero stored wage observations**, while the source reports `ok: true`, `error: null`. Pass the same body with those IDs added to the requested set and the existing parser accepts all three metrics for each county. This is a hypothetical recovery test, not a claim that BLS currently discloses these counties.

**Impact:** the stated objective of building local vintages fails precisely when a new market first becomes measurable. The published mechanical-trade payload attributes an ingestion omission to source suppression; this block is data-only in this PR.

**Correction:** retain the full market roster as collection candidates, with an explicit never-yet-disclosed policy or a discovery path that records valid newly disclosed cells. Add a bounded recheck date if exclusion must temporarily remain. Preserve nulls while cells are actually suppressed. A regression test should show suppressed input producing no observations, then disclosed input entering the store and the market payload without manual registry editing.

### 2. P3 — #125 collects an overlapping aggregate where a separate mechanical wage measure is available

**Location:** `pipeline/publish/labor.py:48–49`, `config/series.json:427`.

The second wage row uses building equipment contractors (`CES2023820003`, NAICS 2382), which includes the electrical contractors already shown in the first row. It is accurately labeled, so this is **a source-selection improvement rather than a numerical defect**. But it does not supply an independent mechanical counterpart to the electrical wage series, despite the parallel 238212/238222 county collection.

Direct BLS API verification on the review date confirmed these August 2026 observations:

| Series | Current $/hour | August 2025 $/hour | Calculated YoY |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Electrical contractors — CES2023821003 | 43.61 | 41.04 | 6.26% |
| Building equipment aggregate — CES2023820003 | 43.13 | 41.04 | 5.09% |
| Plumbing/HVAC contractors — CES2023822003 | 42.44 | 40.62 | 4.48% |

**Correction:** collect/display `CES2023822003` as a separate plumbing/HVAC measure; retain 2382 only if a parent aggregate serves a defined purpose. The 0.61 percentage-point difference in August illustrates why the broader measure cannot stand in for mechanical labor pressure. The mechanical series is still national and includes residential work. BLS [industry definitions](https://www.bls.gov/cew/classifications/industry/industry-titles.htm) establish the parent/child relationship. The evidence JSON records the API endpoint, response hash, and observed values; these are current-vintage figures, not first-release historical values.

### 3. P3 — #125 needs clearer boundaries between craft earnings, operations, and contextual hiring indicators

**Location:** `site/src/app/labor/page.tsx:202,225–235`; `pipeline/publish/labor.py:46–64`.

The individual row names are generally accurate, but the common data-center-trades framing gives unlike measures similar apparent relevance. The hourly earnings series cover all employees in contractor establishments, including office and supervisory staff; they are not electrician/plumber wage quotes. Earnings can move with employee mix, overtime and shifts, and exclude several employer labor-cost components. [BLS CES definitions](https://www.bls.gov/opub/hom/ces/concepts.htm) explicitly distinguish earnings from wage rates and total labor costs.

Indeed's electrical engineering category contains electrical and controls engineers. It does not identify the electrical installation crew represented by the contractor industry series. Installation/maintenance postings and the corresponding ECI occupation aggregate are also broad. The [Indeed category examples](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hiring-lab/job_postings_tracker/master/sector-job-title-examples.csv) and [BLS occupation definition](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2023/may/oes490000.htm) support these boundaries. No proposed series identifies AI-specific demand.

**Correction:** group the rows into contractor earnings, construction employment, fixed-mix wage controls, and contextual hiring signals; add concise scope qualifiers. Engineering and maintenance are useful for design/commissioning/operations context, but should not be presented as a direct installation-worker shortage measure. This is an interpretation improvement, not evidence that those feeds are unsuitable for all uses.

### 4. P3 — #125's committed browser test never exercises the new table

The committed artifact has no `dc_trades` block. The new e2e test expects zero tables and returns immediately in that case. Thus its green CI result does not cover populated rows, values, comparisons, dates, or attribution. The absence branch is appropriate backward-compatibility coverage, but needs a separate populated fixture path. The new formatting functions also have no focused unit coverage.

**Correction:** add a populated artifact fixture or deterministic fixture generation before a focused CI build/browser run. Assert ten intended rows and meaningful amounts/dates, not only the count derived from the same artifact. Cover missing value/YoY and all quarter labels. This is a **coverage limitation**: the independent populated-fixture run in this review passed and revealed no rendering defect.

### 5. P3 — Indeed revision assumptions in the PR description are outdated

**Location:** PR description, Store growth note.

#125 says Indeed seasonal factors are fixed to 2017–2019 and uses that to downplay restatements. The [current Indeed repository methodology](https://github.com/hiring-lab/job_postings_tracker#methodology) describes a November 2024 update, preceding-three-year seasonal estimates, and revised historical numbers. FRED still shows older methodological text. Correct the rationale and expect revisions; the existing full-history fetch and append-on-value-change storage are already helpful controls. This discrepancy is not proof of a current numerical mismatch or a reason to discard Indeed. No licensing conclusion is made in this review.

## Assessment of relevance to AI infrastructure


| Measure | Appropriate role | Limitation that must remain visible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| County QCEW 238212 and 238222 | Most relevant local contractor-industry measures in this change; nonresidential electrical and mechanical labor context | Quarterly lag, disclosure gaps, contractor establishment geography, and average weekly earnings; not on-site crew counts or hourly bid rates |
| National CES electrical plus plumbing/HVAC earnings | Monthly monitoring of relevant contractor earnings | National, residential and nonresidential work mixed, all employees, composition/overtime effects |
| Construction ECI wages | A useful check on wage inflation with fixed industry/occupation weights | Broad construction scope; wages only, not full contractor pricing. [BLS ECI method](https://www.bls.gov/eci/factsheets/eci-reweighting-and-recoding.htm) |
| Nonresidential versus residential specialty jobs | Distinguishes diverging construction segments | Nonresidential includes many activities besides data centers; employment is an equilibrium outcome, not unmet demand |
| ADP construction | A second payroll-source check on broad construction direction | Not a specialty or DC measure; ADP is annually benchmarked to QCEW, so do not treat agreement as wholly independent evidence. [FRED source notes](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/ADPMINDCONNERSA) |
| Indeed postings / installation-maintenance ECI | Context for hiring intent and broader operations/technical labor | Different occupation universes, broad industries, postings are not filled jobs or worker supply |

Keep the display-only approach. Before any index change, evaluate historical stability, revisions, missingness, and explanatory value against a defined project labor scope. The current basket already contains a 15% construction-earnings leg plus 9% electrical and 6% plumbing/HVAC contractor PPI legs. Contractor output prices and employee earnings are different quantities; replacing the PPI legs with wages would require revisiting scope, overlap and weights. A more relevant series name alone does not justify an index swap.
