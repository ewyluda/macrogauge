## F1 — P2: Preserve observation dates before attributing the BBB yield move

**Location:** `site/src/lib/ratesHeadline.ts:22–27`; related display omissions at `site/src/app/rates/page.tsx:123–125` and `site/src/app/datacenter/page.tsx:224–232`.

The new takeaway divides `bbb_oas.chg_1y` by `bbb_yield.chg_1y` without checking the dates of either comparison. Each `_level()` computes its own annual change against its own latest observation, and FRED collection deliberately tolerates per-series failures. The store therefore supports a newly updated yield beside a carried-forward spread. The helper's input type drops `as_of` altogether, so it cannot distinguish that case.

**Reproduction:** the actual writer, supplied with offline observations, produces a schema-valid artifact with Treasury and BBB yield readings dated October 6, 2026, while BBB OAS ends September 8, 2026. BBB yield changes from 5.00% to 6.19% over its October-to-October window; OAS changes from 0.94% to 1.02% over its September-to-September window. The actual helper nevertheless returns:

> The 10-year Treasury is 5.30% (+110bp on the year) and BBB corporate debt yields 6.19% (+119bp); almost all of that is rates, not credit: the BBB spread moved +8bp

Those are different annual windows; the September spread change cannot establish what drove the October yield change. Re-publishing this artifact with the current run's timestamp does not trigger `StaleBanner`, which detects failed artifact refreshes rather than aged observations. The new BBB yield KPI also omits its `as_of`, and every new capital-strip KPI omits its observation date. Rendering the fixture confirms that neither supplies the missing qualification. Even the committed Treasury-only strip hides the October 5 observation date visible on `/rates`.

**Correction:** calculate any comparison on a shared end date and shared year-ago baseline in the publisher, or present independently dated changes without attribution when their windows differ. Carry those dates into the helper and the new KPI contexts. A same-date comparison should still be described as an indication: [FRED's OAS methodology](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BAMLC0A4CBBB) uses constituent option-adjusted spreads against a spot Treasury curve; it does not establish an exact additive decomposition into the displayed constant-maturity 10-year yield and OAS.

This is a reproduced supported-data failure mode, not a claim that a live BBB fetch is currently failing. The committed artifact predates all four new series.

## F2 — P2: Label the financing measures as benchmarks rather than a build's borrowing cost

**Location:** `site/src/app/datacenter/page.tsx:222–232`; the same framing appears in `site/src/app/rates/page.tsx:114–117`.

The new section is introduced as “what a build is financed at,” the Treasury card says it is the base for every fixed-rate loan, and the SOFR card calls the raw average “floating-rate construction debt.” The page supplies no project, loan tenor, contractual benchmark, lender margin, fees, or borrower credit terms. Those labels give the new numbers a stronger economic meaning than their sources support.

- [SOFR30DAYAVG](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SOFR30DAYAVG) is a compounded average over the preceding 30 days. [CME Term SOFR](https://www.cmegroup.com/market-data/cme-group-benchmark-administration/term-sofr.html) is a separate forward-looking benchmark with specified tenors. The registry correctly names the average, but the rendered pages do not explain this distinction or the absent borrowing spread.
- [BAMLC0A4CBBBEY](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BAMLC0A4CBBBEY) measures effective yield on a BBB-rated US corporate bond index. It is not a quoted financing rate for a data-center construction loan.

**Reproduction:** rendering the actual hub with a schema-valid fixture puts `3.79%` immediately above “floating-rate construction debt,” beside the section's financing-cost claim, with no benchmark/proxy qualification. The committed page already makes the universal fixed-rate-loan claim for the 10-year Treasury.

**Correction:** call this a financing-benchmark strip and the BBB series a corporate-bond benchmark. Describe SOFR as the backward-looking 30-day compounded base rate, distinct from Term SOFR and exclusive of loan spreads and fees. State that actual financing depends on the loan's terms; retain the useful market series without presenting them as a project's cost of debt.

## F3 — P2: Make the new browser test accept valid small-change and missing-data states

**Location:** `site/e2e/research-refresh.spec.ts:430–434`.

The new test skips only when `credit.bbb_yield` is absent, then always requires both a BBB card and a `rates-takeaway` element. After this writer runs, `bbb_yield` always exists as an object, including when its value is null. Independently, `ratesHeadline()` intentionally omits the takeaway when the annual yield move is below 0.1 percentage point or either annual change is unavailable.

Three offline fixtures produced by the actual writer and validated against the actual schema demonstrate the mismatch:

| Valid artifact state | Test skips? | BBB card exists? | Takeaway exists? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BBB yield 6.19%, annual change +5bp | No | Yes | No |
| No BBB yield observations | No | No | No |
| BBB yield present, no year-ago base | No | Yes | No |

The assertions therefore fail on supported application output. This can turn future CI runs red solely because a market move is small or a new source is unavailable. The current all-green run does not exercise these assertions: the committed pre-series artifact reaches the skip.

**Correction:** keep structural assertions independent of market outcomes, and condition the expected card/takeaway on available values and the documented display rule. Add deterministic fixture coverage for complete, small-change, missing-level, and missing-baseline states so those branches are exercised before the next daily publication.

## F4 — P3: Include the new IG and BBB histories in the rates CSV

**Location:** new history/plot additions in `pipeline/publish/rates.py:28–31` and `site/src/app/rates/page.tsx:222–223`; omitted consumer update in `site/src/lib/exportSpecs.ts:11–15`.

The PR publishes `history.ig_oas` and `history.bbb_oas` and adds both lines to the credit chart, but the existing `RATES_HISTORY_CSV` recipe still enumerates only the old columns. Calling the actual `rowsFromSpec()` and `toCsv()` against a new schema-valid artifact produces:

```text
date,dgs3mo,dgs2,dgs10,t5yie,t10yie,hy_oas,dollar,spread_2s10s,spread_3m10y,real_10y
```

**Impact:** users can see the new spread histories on the page and in JSON but cannot obtain them through its rates-history CSV export. Existing export tests compare against the old column list, so they pass without detecting the omission.

**Correction:** add the two optional history columns to the recipe and verify populated values for new artifacts and empty cells for older artifacts. The existing CSV resolver already handles absent paths.
